# Foundation of Information Systems  

---

## 1. Networks
- Networks connect multiple computers and devices.  
- Benefits:
  - Share printers and other peripherals.
  - Share programs and data easily.
  - Enable email and communication tools.
  - Provide centralized backups for important files.
  - Allow access to shared databases.

---

## 2. Communication Systems
- Electronic systems that transfer data between locations.  
- Core to enabling network connectivity across distances.

---

## 3. Basic Elements of Communication Systems
1. **Sending and Receiving Devices**: Computers or communication devices.  
2. **Connection Devices**: Interfaces between sender and receiver.  
3. **Transmission Specifications**: Rules and procedures that coordinate data exchange.  
4. **Communication Channel**: The medium (wired or wireless) that carries the message.

---

## 4. Network Architecture
- Defines how networks are structured and resources are shared.  
- Two main aspects:
  - **Topology**: Physical arrangement of devices.  
  - **Strategy**: How resources and information are managed.

---

## 5. Network Topologies
- **Ring Network**: Each node connects to exactly two others, forming a continuous loop.  
- **Star Network**: All devices connect directly to a central switch.  
- **Tree Network**: Devices connect hierarchically to a central node or through subordinate nodes.  
- **Bus Network**: All devices share a single communication line.

---

## 6. Network Strategies
- **Client/Server**:
  - Central server provides and manages services.
  - Clients request access from the server.
- **Peer-to-Peer (P2P)**:
  - All nodes are equal, acting as both client and server.
  - Cheaper but less efficient for large offices.
  - File-sharing is harder to control, and versioning issues arise.

---

## 7. Connection Devices
- Networks require devices to convert signals.  
- **Modem** (Modulator-Demodulator):
  - Converts digital to analog (modulation) and analog to digital (demodulation).
  - Measured in Mbps (megabits per second).

---

## 8. Digital and Analog Signals
- **Digital signals**: Represented by discrete values (0s and 1s).  
- **Analog signals**: Continuous waves, used in older communication systems.  
- Conversion is essential for data transmission across different media.

---

## 9. Specialized Terms in Networks
- **Node**: Any connected device.  
- **Client**: Requests resources from other nodes.  
- **Server**: Provides resources to clients.  
- **Directory Server**: Manages network resources.  
- **Host**: A computer accessible across a network.

---

## 10. IP Address (Internet Protocol)
- Unique identifier stored in 4 bytes.  
- Divided into:
  - **Network Address**: Identifies the network.  
  - **Host Number**: Identifies the device within the network.

---

## 11. Domain Name System (DNS)
- Converts hostnames into IP addresses.  
- Works as a distributed database:
  - If one server cannot resolve a name, it queries another.  
- **Hostname Example**: `csc.villanova.edu`.  
- **Top-Level Domains (TLDs)**: The final section of a domain (e.g., `.com`, `.org`, `.edu`).  
- **Country Codes**: Domains tied to countries (e.g., `.uk`, `.eg`, `.fr`).
