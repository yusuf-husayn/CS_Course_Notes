# Introduction to Information Systems

## Data
- Raw material  
- Numbers or letters with no precise context or meaning  

## Information
- Data processed with knowledge  
- Data + Relevance + Purpose  
- Organized, meaningful, useful interpretation of data  

### Characteristics of Valuable Information
- Relevant  
- Complete  
- Accurate  
- Current/Timely  
- Economical  
- Accessible  

### Determining Value
- Time saved, lower costs  
- More accurate forecasts  
- Improved service  
- Often difficult to quantify  
- Payback period?  

## Knowledge
- Guidelines and rules to select, organize, manipulate data  
- Awareness and understanding of information and how to use it  
- Internalized information + ability to utilize it  

## Transformation
- Data → establish relationships → Information  
- Information + internalization → Knowledge

# Systems

## Definition
Components that work together to:
- Accept input  
- Process it  
- Produce output in an organized manner  

## Components
- **Inputs**: Gathering raw data  
- **Processing**: Converting data into outputs  
- **Outputs**: Producing useful information  
- **Feedback**: Using output to adjust input or processing  
- **Control**: Guides and regulates system activities  
- **Environment**: External factors affecting the system  
- **Boundaries & Interfaces**: Define scope and connections  

## Types
- **Physical vs Abstract**: Tangible entities vs conceptual models  
- **Open vs Closed**: Interaction with environment vs isolated  
- **Deterministic vs Probabilistic**: Predictable vs uncertain  

## Performance
- **Efficiency**: Output ÷ Input  
- **Effectiveness**: Achieved ÷ Goal  

## Variables
- **System Variable**: Controllable (e.g., selling price)  
- **System Parameter**: Not controllable (e.g., raw material cost)

# Information Systems (IS)

## Definition
Components that process data and produce information to support problem-solving and decision-making.  

## Components of a CBIS
1. Hardware  
2. Software (OS + Applications)  
3. Databases  
4. Telecommunications/Networks  
5. People  
6. Procedures  

## IS Components in Organizations
- Data: Input  
- Hardware: Computers, peripherals  
- Software: Instructions for input, processing, output  
- Communication networks: Data transmission  
- People: Professionals + End users  
- Procedures: Policies, rules, strategies  

---

# Types of Business Information Systems

## Transaction Processing Systems (TPS)
- Records daily business transactions  
- Used by operational management  
- Highly structured decisions  
- Examples: POS, Payroll, Stock Control, Airline booking  

## Management Information Systems (MIS)
- Input from TPS → summarized reports  
- Supports tactical managers with semi-structured decisions  
- Examples: Sales management, Budgeting, HR systems  

## Decision Support Systems (DSS)
- Supports non-routine, complex decisions  
- Uses TPS, MIS, and external data  
- Compares alternatives, answers “What if” questions  
- Examples: Financial planning, Bank loan management  

## Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP)
- Integrated programs for company-wide operations  
- Coordinate planning, inventory, production, ordering  

## Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Expert Systems
- Mimic human expertise, recognize patterns  
- Used in Amazon (recommendations), Google (search), Facebook (friend suggestions)  
- Expert systems: medical diagnosis, repair, credit evaluation, investments  

## E-Commerce
- Business transactions executed electronically (B2B, B2C)  
- Lowers barriers to entry  

## Workflow Systems
- Rule-based coordination of tasks and processes

# Strategy Levels and IS Examples

- **Operational**: Day-to-day tasks, structured decisions (cashiers, nurses, payroll)  
- **Tactical**: Department heads, supervisors, semi-structured decisions  
- **Strategic**: Senior managers, unstructured decisions, long-term planning

# Systems Development

## Objectives
- Manageable process  
- Predictable costs and timing  

## Steps
1. **Investigation**: Understand problem/opportunity  
2. **Analysis**: Define problems and opportunities  
3. **Design**: Plan how system will work  
4. **Implementation**: Build or acquire components, put into operation 
5. **Maintenance & Review**: Ensure system adapts to changing needs
