 >[!Question] 00
 >What is the main role of a SOC (Security Operations Center)?

- [ ] To develop new malware

- [x] To monitor and defend an organization’s systems and networks
>[!Reason]
>A Security Operations Center (SOC) is a centralized unit responsible for the continuous monitoring, analysis, and response to cybersecurity incidents. Its primary goal is to maintain the organization's security posture by detecting threats in real-time and defending the infrastructure against attacks, rather than focusing on offensive tasks or purely administrative reporting.

- [ ] To conduct penetration tests only

- [ ] To write forensic reports

>[!Question] 01
>Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting all information, whether digital or physical

- [ ] صحيح

- [x] خطأ
>[!Reason]
>**Cybersecurity** specifically focuses on protecting internet-connected systems, networks, and data from _digital_ attacks. The broader term that covers protecting _all_ information, including physical records (like paper files in a locked cabinet) and digital data, is **Information Security (InfoSec)**.

>[!Question] 02
>Offensive security involves breaking into computer systems, exploiting software bugs, and finding loopholes in applications to gain unauthorized access. The goal is to understand hacker tactics and enhance our system defences.

- [x] صحيح
>[!Reason]
>Offensive security (often referred to as **Ethical Hacking** or **Red Teaming**) takes a proactive approach to cybersecurity. Instead of waiting for an attack to happen, security professionals simulate real-world attacks to identify vulnerabilities and weaknesses in the system. By understanding how an attacker thinks and operates, organizations can patch these holes and strengthen their defenses before malicious actors can exploit them.

>[!Question] 03
>Which of the following is NOT a common type of malware?

- [ ] virus

- [ ] worm

- [x] firewall
>[!Reason]
>A **firewall** is a network security system (software or hardware) designed to monitor and control incoming and outgoing network traffic based on security rules. It is a defensive tool used to _protect_ against threats.
>
>In contrast, **viruses**, **worms**, and **ransomware** are all types of **malware** (malicious software) created to harm or exploit computers and networks.

- [ ] Ransomware

>[!Question] 04
>What is the key difference between a Penetration Tester and a Red Teamer?

- [x] Penetration Testers focus on broad testing, Red Teamers simulate realistic attacks
>[!Reason]
>**Penetration Testing** is typically a time-boxed assessment where the goal is to find and report as many vulnerabilities as possible within a specific system or network (like a security audit).
>
>**Red Teaming**, on the other hand, is a full-scope, objective-based simulation of a real-world adversary. Red Teamers use stealth and multiple vectors (including social engineering and physical security) to achieve a specific goal—like accessing a sensitive database—without being caught, testing the organization's detection and response capabilities.

- [ ] Both do exactly the same tasks

- [ ] Penetration Testers work only with malware, Red Teamers do not

- [ ] Red Teamers only test physical security

>[!Question] 05
>A Security Engineer designs and builds secure systems and infrastructure.

- [x] صحيح
>[!Reason]
>A **Security Engineer** is proactive, focusing on _designing_ and _building_ secure systems from the ground up (often called "Security by Design"). They implement safeguards like firewalls, encryption, and secure network architectures to prevent attacks before they happen.
>
>In contrast, a **Security Analyst** typically monitors for and responds to attacks _after_ systems are built, and a **Penetration Tester** tries to break them.

>[!Question] 06
>A Digital Forensic Examiner works with recovering and analyzing evidence from digital devices

- [x] صحيح
>[!Reason]
>A **Digital Forensic Examiner** is a specialized role focused on the recovery, investigation, and analysis of material found in digital devices (like computers, phones, and networks), often in relation to computer crime. Their work is critical for legal proceedings, as they must preserve the integrity of evidence while uncovering details about a security incident or crime.
